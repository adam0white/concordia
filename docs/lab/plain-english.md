# Plain English

Use full grammatical sentences for instructions, narration, dialogue, states and errors. Include the articles and verbs a reader needs. Labels and action buttons may name their action directly.

Say literally what happens. Use one name for each thing. Explain a game term when it first appears. Introduce one new idea per screen where possible. Use familiar words; aim for grade 7 instructions. Dialogue may be richer but must remain grammatical. State uncertainty clearly: “Nora looks tired.” Cut words the player does not need.

Keep the setting, places and personal names. Every sentence must be understandable without Turkish. Use English for common nouns. On first use, call the kapıcı “the building’s caretaker”; later use “caretaker”. Keep at most two flavour words per game and explain each within its first sentence. Faith stays quiet background; source faith wording from Human Framework’s `research/` and do not invent rulings. Preserve rules, difficulty and recorded history. Use a display projection when changing a stored sentence would affect replay. Technical diagnostics require exact `?lab=1`. Ordinary errors must explain recovery through visible actions.

Reading checks flag source candidates, including diagnostics and historical keys. They do not measure rendered reading grade. Inspect actual first scenes and alternate states before delivery.
